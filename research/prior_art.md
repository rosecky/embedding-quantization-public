# Prior-Art and Novelty Sweep — Threshold-Aware Joint Quantization of Embedding Encoders + Output Codes

Compiled 2026-09-02. Scope: retrieval-oriented vector/hash quantization, joint encoder+index training, extreme low-bit (≤2-bit) embedding encoders, Matryoshka/nested representations, asymmetric dense retrieval, and multilingual vocabulary/embedding-table compression. Searched via arXiv, ACL Anthology, OpenReview, Hugging Face, GitHub, and vendor engineering blogs (Google Research, Elastic, Vespa, Mixedbread, Intel).

**Caveat on method**: findings below come from web search snippets and automated page-fetch summaries (arXiv abstract/HTML pages, HF model cards, GitHub READMEs), not from manually reading full PDFs end-to-end. Numbers and formulas are reported as retrieved; anything that looks under-specified is flagged rather than guessed. Two 2026 papers (CQ-CiM, ASH) had their PDFs fetched and summarized by an intermediate model — treat their loss-function descriptions as paraphrase, not verbatim quotation, and re-check the primary source before relying on exact wording in a paper draft.

## 1. Executive summary

- **Nobody found is doing exactly this.** No 2020-2026 paper explicitly frames retrieval quantization as "preserve the superlevel set / decision boundary of a teacher relevance threshold, with an asymmetric false-positive guard, without preserving the teacher's coordinate system, jointly across weights and output index codes." That specific combination is not in the literature we found.
- **The individual ingredients all exist separately**, and some pairs of them co-occur:
  - *Score-aware, non-reconstruction loss for codes*: ScaNN's anisotropic loss (Guo et al. 2020) already penalizes parallel vs. orthogonal residual error asymmetrically, motivated by "large inner products matter more" — but it is a similarity-magnitude-aware loss, not a threshold-crossing / classification-style loss, and it ships no false-positive-specific term.
  - *Joint encoder+index training with a ranking loss*: JPQ, Distill-VQ, RepCONC, Poeem, MoPQ, LibVQ (2021-2022) all jointly train a dense encoder and PQ codebook using ranking/contrastive/KD objectives instead of reconstruction — closest prior art to "joint encoder + index code" training, but none of them frame the objective as threshold-crossing/superlevel-set preservation, and none of them are ternary-weight encoders.
  - *Explicit top-k/ranking-preserving objective for a fixed embedding space (index-only)*: BEBR (Tencent, 2023), ASH (2026), CQ-CiM (2026), and the general deep-hashing "ranking-preserving" family (HashNet, DPSH, Deep Cauchy Hashing) all optimize codes for ranking or Hamming-radius thresholds rather than reconstruction — the closest single-sided analogues of "threshold + local ranking," but they touch only the index/codes, not a *jointly ternarized* encoder, and none report an explicit asymmetric false-positive guard distinct from ScaNN-style weighting.
  - *Extreme low-bit (ternary) embedding encoder, trained with distillation*: Microsoft's BitEmbed / BitNet Text Embeddings (2606.25674, 2026) is the closest direct comparator for the "encoder" half — W1.58A8 backbone + flexible 1/2/4/8/16-bit *output* quantization, trained with contrastive + full-distribution similarity-KD + attention-relation KD. But BitEmbed's similarity-distillation term is a **full KL divergence over the entire in-batch similarity distribution** — it does not privilege a relevance threshold, does not have a top-k/superlevel-set-specific term, does not have an asymmetric false-positive penalty, and does not jointly train an ANN index (its "output quantization" is per-dimension scalar/binary, not a joint rate-constrained PQ/OPQ/graph index). It also explicitly preserves the teacher's coordinate system (cosine-similarity distillation to the FP16 teacher's own geometry), which is the opposite of "teacher-coordinate-system-free."
- **What appears genuinely open, based on this sweep**: (a) a *joint* rate-constrained objective that quantizes both encoder weights AND output codes under a single threshold/top-k-preserving loss with an explicit asymmetric false-positive term; (b) any paper that argues explicitly for abandoning the teacher's coordinate system (i.e., not preserving cosine values below threshold, only preserving crossing decisions) as a deliberate design choice rather than an accepted side effect of lossy compression.
- **Adjacent theoretical result worth knowing**: Weller et al. (Google DeepMind/JHU, "On the Theoretical Limitations of Embedding-Based Retrieval," Aug 2025, arXiv:2508.21038) proves that for fixed embedding dimension d, some top-k document subsets are simply not realizable by any single-vector dot-product embedding — a hard ceiling independent of quantization. This bounds how much a threshold/top-k-preserving objective can promise at very low output bit-budgets, and should be cited as a caveat, not a competitor.
- **Practical bottom line for the program**: the "joint W-and-codes, threshold+top-k, false-positive-guard, coordinate-free" combination looks like a genuinely novel synthesis, but every one of its four pieces has a close single- or double-feature precedent (see table in §2 and §4). Any paper/claim must be scoped against JPQ/Distill-VQ/RepCONC (joint training precedent), ScaNN/BEBR/ASH (asymmetric/ranking-preserving code precedent), and BitEmbed (ternary-encoder + flexible-output-bits precedent) explicitly, or a reviewer familiar with any of the three will flag prior art.

## 3. Per-work notes

### 3.1 ScaNN / Anisotropic Vector Quantization (Guo et al., ICML 2020) and SOAR (2023)
- **Citation**: Guo, R., Sun, P., Lindgren, E., Geng, Q., Simcha, D., Chern, F., Kumar, S. "Accelerating Large-Scale Inference with Anisotropic Vector Quantization." ICML 2020. arXiv:1908.10396.
- **Venue/year**: ICML 2020 (arXiv Aug 2019, v5 Dec 2020).
- **Mechanism**: Departs from reconstruction-error minimization for PQ codebooks. Introduces "a family of anisotropic quantization loss functions" motivated by the observation that for maximum inner product search (MIPS), datapoints with large inner products to the query matter more; the loss weights the *parallel* component of a datapoint's quantization residual (along the direction to the query) much more heavily than the *orthogonal* component.
- **Optimizes**: A weighted, direction-dependent reconstruction loss (not classification/threshold loss) intended to make quantized inner-product estimates more accurate exactly where it matters for top-k MIPS retrieval.
- **Bit-widths**: PQ-based; typical ScaNN configurations use per-subvector codebooks (commonly 4–8 bits per subvector) — this is index-side only, the embedding model itself is untouched.
- **Reported results**: State-of-the-art recall/QPS on Glove, SIFT, and other ann-benchmarks.org datasets (exact numbers not independently re-verified in this sweep — see the ICML PDF for tables).
- **Code**: Open source, part of google-research/google-research (scann folder), pip-installable `scann`.
- **Follow-up — SOAR** (Sun, Guo, Kumar, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2404.00774): "Spilling with Orthogonality-Amplified Residuals" — trains multiple redundant per-point representations with an orthogonality-amplified residual loss so that when one representation performs poorly for a query, a correlated backup representation compensates; achieved SOTA on two 2023 Big-ANN benchmark tracks.
- **Relation to our direction**: This is the field's canonical "score-aware, not reconstruction, quantization loss," and the closest single precedent for "don't treat all coordinate error equally." But it (a) never touches the encoder, only the index; (b) has no explicit relevance threshold — it weights by continuous inner-product magnitude, not by a binary above/below-threshold decision; (c) has no false-positive-specific asymmetric term (its asymmetry is parallel-vs-orthogonal error, not positive-vs-negative-pair error). Any claim of novelty must explicitly distinguish "asymmetric by direction" (ScaNN) from "asymmetric by relevance-threshold side" (our proposal).

### 3.2 JPQ (Zhan et al., CIKM 2021) and Distill-VQ (Xiao et al., SIGIR 2022)
- **JPQ citation**: Zhan, J., Mao, J., Liu, Y., Guo, J., Zhang, M., Ma, S. "Jointly Optimizing Query Encoder and Product Quantization to Improve Retrieval Performance." CIKM 2021. arXiv:2108.00644.
- **Mechanism**: Trains the query encoder and a PQ codebook end-to-end via (1) a ranking-oriented loss, (2) direct PQ centroid optimization, (3) end-to-end negative sampling — explicitly rejecting the standard two-stage "train encoder, then PQ-compress" pipeline because reconstruction-oriented PQ training is inconsistent with ranking quality.
- **Optimizes**: Ranking loss (contrastive/triplet-style over labeled query-document relevance), not reconstruction MSE.
- **Bit-widths**: 30× index compression reported (e.g., 8 sub-quantizers × 8 bits ≈ typical OPQ/PQ setups); document encoder is left in float precision, only the *query*-side pipeline and PQ codebook are optimized jointly.
- **Reported results**: Matches/exceeds brute-force dense retrieval baselines at 30× compression, 10× CPU speedup, 2× GPU speedup (per paper abstract).
- **Code**: github.com/jingtaozhan/JPQ.
- **Distill-VQ citation**: Xiao, S., Liu, Z., Han, W., Zhang, J., Lian, D., Gong, Y., Chen, Q., Yang, F., Sun, H., Shao, Y., Xie, X. "Distill-VQ: Learning Retrieval Oriented Vector Quantization By Distilling Knowledge from Dense Embeddings." SIGIR 2022. arXiv:2204.00185.
- **Mechanism**: Unifies IVF and PQ training under a knowledge-distillation framework — the full-precision dense embeddings act as a "teacher" producing query-document relevance predictions, and the VQ module (IVF assignment + PQ codes) is the "student" trained to reproduce those relevance predictions, using large amounts of *unlabeled* data (no need for human relevance labels).
- **Optimizes**: KD loss matching VQ-based relevance scores to dense-embedding relevance scores (not reconstruction).
- **Code**: Distributed via github.com/staoxiao/LibVQ (a general dense-retrieval-oriented VQ library that also implements JPQ, RepCONC-style training).
- **Relation to our direction**: JPQ and Distill-VQ are the two clearest precedents for "jointly train an encoder(-side component) and an index code under a task loss instead of reconstruction." Distill-VQ's "teacher predicts relevance, student reproduces relevance" framing is conceptually adjacent to "preserve threshold-crossing decisions, not coordinates" — but both use continuous ranking/relevance-score matching (effectively softly weighting the whole score distribution or top-ranked results), not an explicit binary threshold-crossing / superlevel-set-preservation loss, and neither ternarizes the encoder's weights or defines an asymmetric false-positive-specific penalty. Neither reaches into weight quantization at all — the "encoder" side they touch is architecture/routing (which vectors go to which IVF cell), not bit-width.

### 3.3 Supervised/deep hashing (HashNet, DPSH, Deep Cauchy Hashing) and dense-retrieval binary codes (BPR)
- **HashNet** (Cao et al., ICCV 2017): learns exact binary codes from imbalanced similarity data via a continuation method that anneals a smoothed tanh-like activation toward the true sign() function, avoiding vanishing gradients from directly optimizing sign().
- **DPSH — Deep Pairwise-Supervised Hashing** (Li, Wang, Kang, 2016): first method for simultaneous CNN feature learning + hash code learning from pairwise labels, using a likelihood loss over pairwise similarity.
- **Deep Cauchy Hashing (DCH)** (Cao, Long, Wang, Yu, CVPR 2018): designs a pairwise cross-entropy loss based on the Cauchy distribution that "penalizes significantly on similar pairs with Hamming distance larger than a given Hamming-radius threshold." Reported to outperform HashNet by 7.3–10.4 MAP points at matched code length on standard image-hashing benchmarks. **This is the closest classical precedent for an explicit "threshold" in a retrieval-quantization loss** — it directly penalizes similar pairs that cross a fixed Hamming-distance threshold, which is structurally similar in spirit to "threshold-crossing risk," though phrased in Hamming space with a heavy-tailed distributional weighting rather than as a superlevel-set/false-positive-guard formulation, and it operates on images/CNNs, not multilingual text embeddings.
- **BPR — Binary Passage Retriever** (Yamada, Asai, Hajishirzi, ACL 2021 short paper, arXiv:2106.00882): integrates a learning-to-hash layer (sign() activation) into DPR's dual encoder, trained with a **multi-task objective**: a hashing loss for efficient binary-code candidate generation, plus a separate ranking loss on continuous vectors for accurate reranking of the binary-retrieved candidates. Reduces DPR's memory footprint from 65 GB to 2 GB on NQ/TriviaQA "without a loss of accuracy" (per abstract).
- **Code**: github.com/studio-ousia/bpr; also integrated as a baseline in the BEIR benchmark suite (beir-cellar/beir).
- **Relation to our direction**: The deep-hashing "ranking-preserving"/"threshold" family (esp. DCH) is the most direct classical precedent for framing a retrieval-code loss around a distance/similarity **threshold** rather than reconstruction, and BPR is the most direct precedent for a **two-stage candidate-then-rerank** design that tolerates coarse binary-code errors as long as the reranking stage catches them — structurally close to "students don't need exact values below threshold." Neither, however, frames the objective as encoder+index *joint rate-constrained* optimization, neither ternarizes encoder weights, and neither has an explicit asymmetric false-positive-only penalty (DCH's Cauchy weighting is symmetric in that it penalizes both similar-pairs-too-far and dissimilar-pairs-too-close, tuned by one radius parameter, not a distinct one-sided "hard negative just below threshold" guard).

### 3.4 REDH and "Binary Neural Network Hashing" (BNNH)
- **BNNH citation**: "Binary Neural Network Hashing for Image Retrieval," SIGIR 2021 (Zhang, Wanqian et al.), dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3404835.3462896.
- **Mechanism**: A deep hashing method reported to "outperform state-of-the-art binarization methods by large margins" on three benchmarks; compares against a baseline called **"REDH"** in its Table 3 MAP comparison, alongside a plain CNN baseline.
- **REDH — not independently confirmed.** We could not retrieve REDH's own paper (title, authors, venue) — the ACM full text was behind a 403 and no independent search surfaced an expandable citation for "REDH" as a standalone method. It is referenced only as a baseline inside the BNNH paper. **State explicitly: REDH's exact citation could not be verified in this sweep; do not cite it without locating the original paper (possibly a Chinese-conference or workshop hashing paper with a similar acronym — "Region/Residual/Robust-Enhanced Discriminative Hashing" are unverified guesses, not confirmed expansions).**
- **Relation to our direction**: BNNH itself is a mainstream discriminative/pairwise deep-hashing paper for images (not text/multilingual retrieval), with no evident threshold- or rate-constrained joint-encoder framing beyond the general deep-hashing paradigm already covered in §3.3.

### 3.5 Efficient Ternary Weight Embedding Model (Chen, Wu, Zhang, Li, Zhang, Zhang, 2024)
- **Citation**: "Efficient Ternary Weight Embedding Model: Bridging Scalability and Performance." arXiv:2411.15438 (Nov 2024).
- **Mechanism**: A fine-tuning framework that ternarizes the linear layers of a pretrained embedding model, using a "self-taught knowledge distillation" procedure (the paper's own wording; exact formulation not confirmed from the fetched abstract — the mechanism appears to be self-distillation from the model's own full-precision version into its ternarized version, but this was not independently verified against the full PDF).
- **Optimizes / bit-widths**: Ternary (~2-bit-encoded {-1,0,1}) linear-layer weights; activation bit-width and output-embedding bit-width are **not confirmed** from available summaries — this needs a full-PDF read before citing precisely.
- **Reported results**: Claims retained effectiveness with low memory/latency on unspecified "public text and vision datasets," and reports gains when combined with ANN search; exact numbers not retrieved.
- **Code**: A GitHub repo is referenced (`dataparameters/Ternary-Embedding-Models` surfaced in search) but link/contents not independently verified.
- **Relation to our direction**: Closest prior art for "ternarize an embedding model's weights via distillation," pre-dating BitEmbed by over a year, but it does not address output/index-code quantization jointly, and no evidence of threshold/top-k framing was found. Flag as needing a closer read before any priority claim is made relative to it.

### 3.6 TernaryCLIP (2025)
- **Citation**: "TernaryCLIP: Efficiently Compressing Vision-Language Models with Ternary Weights and Distilled Knowledge." arXiv:2510.21879 (Oct 2025).
- **Mechanism**: Ternarizes both the vision and text towers of CLIP using QAT plus a distillation module, compared against a distillation-only "CLIP-KD" baseline.
- **Bit-widths**: Up to 99% of weights ternarized (1.58-bit equivalent).
- **Reported results**: 16.98× compression, 2.3× inference acceleration, 16× storage reduction, 10× memory optimization, 60% sparsity; only 2.17% average performance degradation vs. distillation-only CLIP-KD, evaluated on 41 zero-shot classification/retrieval datasets (per search summary; exact per-dataset numbers not independently pulled).
- **Code**: github.com/zhangsq-nju/TernaryCLIP.
- **Relation to our direction**: Multimodal analogue of ternary-weight embedding compression, structurally similar to BitEmbed/Efficient-Ternary-Weight-Embedding but for CLIP; no evidence found of threshold-aware or output-code-joint objectives — it is a weight-ternarization + generic distillation paper, not a retrieval-functional-objective paper.

### 3.7 Microsoft BitNet Text Embeddings / BitEmbed — closest direct comparator
- **Citation**: Li, Z., Huang, X., Wang, L., Yang, N., Song, T., Xia, Y., Wu, X., Huang, S., Zhang, H., Wei, F., Zhao, D. "BitNet Text Embeddings." arXiv:2606.25674 (submitted 24 Jun 2026, revised 22 Jul 2026).
- **Models**: `microsoft/bitnet-embedding-270m` (Gemma3-270M backbone) and `microsoft/bitnet-embedding-0.6b` (Qwen3-0.6B backbone), both on Hugging Face, MIT license.
- **Architecture (0.6B)**: 28 layers, 16 attention heads (8 KV heads), head dim 128, hidden size 1024, vocab 151,936 (Qwen3 tokenizer), output embedding dim 1024, last-token (EOS) pooling + L2 normalization.
- **Architecture (270M)**: Gemma3 backbone, 18 hidden layers, 4 attention heads (1 KV head — GQA), head dim 256, intermediate size 2048, GELU, Gemma tokenizer (262,144 vocab), 32,768 context length, output embedding dim 640, last-token pooling + L2 norm.
- **Training recipe (3 stages)**:
  1. **Continual contrastive pre-training**: BitNet-converted (ternary weight + SubLN) backbone trained on ~1B text pairs with InfoNCE loss.
  2. **Distillation-based supervised fine-tuning**: contrastive loss (in-batch + 7 hard negatives/query) + **similarity-distribution distillation** (KL divergence between teacher's and student's full softmax similarity distribution over the batch, weight λ_s=0.2) + **attention-relation distillation** (KL divergence between teacher's and student's multi-head attention relation matrices at a chosen layer, weight λ_a=10⁴), temperature τ=0.02.
  3. **Multi-precision embedding training**: the combined loss is additionally averaged/optimized jointly across output precisions {1,2,4,8,16} bits, so a single model produces good embeddings at any of those output bit-widths.
- **Bit-widths**: Backbone weights ternary via absmean quantization to {−Δ,0,+Δ} (1.58-bit); activations 8-bit per-token (W1.58A8); **output embeddings selectable at 1 (sign+absmax), 2/4/8 (uniform scalar with per-vector absmax scale), or 16-bit (fp16, unquantized)**.
- **Reported MTEB(v2, eng) results** (from paper Table 1, per fetched HTML): Qwen3-0.6B FP16 teacher avg 67.95 vs. BitEmbed avg 67.60 (near-parity), at 2.06× CPU throughput (364→830 tok/s); Gemma3-270M FP16 teacher avg 66.71 vs. BitEmbed 66.10, 1.74× CPU throughput speedup. Multilingual MMTEB: Qwen3-based 67.49 (teacher 69.00), Gemma3-based 66.26 (teacher 66.55) — these multilingual numbers match what the HF model cards show directly. Multi-precision degradation on Qwen3-0.6B: 1-bit 64.43, 2-bit 64.90, 4-bit 67.45, 8-bit 67.55, 16-bit 67.60 average — i.e., 16× storage reduction (1-bit) costs ~3.2 MTEB-avg points; retrieval is reported as the *most* quantization-sensitive task category. Ablations show removing both distillation losses costs 2.84 points and "BitNet SFT only" (no continual pretraining, no distillation) collapses to 58.92 — i.e., the paper's own ablation shows distillation is load-bearing, not cosmetic.
- **Code**: Model weights on Hugging Face; the inference framework is `microsoft/BitNet` (bitnet.cpp) and the model cards state bitnet.cpp support plus llama.cpp/GGUF (BF16) support. No separate training-code repository was found.
- **Relation to our direction — this is the paper to position against.** It already does: ternary weights + int8 activations (matches "quantization of an embedding encoder"), flexible low-bit *output* embeddings (matches "output index codes" in spirit, though not a joint ANN-index code — each dimension is quantized independently, no PQ/OPQ/graph-index rate-distortion joint optimization), and a form of distillation from a teacher. **It differs from the proposed direction in exactly the ways that matter**: (1) its similarity-distillation loss is a *full-distribution* KL divergence over all in-batch similarities — every pair's similarity value is targeted, not just threshold-crossing decisions; (2) it has no local top-k / superlevel-set-specific term, no asymmetric false-positive guard, and no notion of "irrelevant pairs below threshold don't matter"; (3) it explicitly preserves the teacher's coordinate system (the whole point of similarity-distribution distillation is to match the teacher's own cosine geometry), the opposite of "teacher-coordinate-system-free"; (4) its output-bit quantization is per-dimension/independent, not a jointly-optimized index code (no PQ/OPQ/graph structure, no rate-distortion coupling between weight bits and code bits). Any paper on the proposed direction must cite BitEmbed as the nearest baseline and show a controlled ablation against exactly this full-KL similarity-distillation objective.

### 3.8 Matryoshka Representation Learning and binary/int8 variants
- **MRL citation**: Kusupati, A. et al. "Matryoshka Representation Learning." NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2205.13147.
- **Mechanism**: Trains nested embeddings so that any dimension-prefix (e.g., first 64 of 768 dims) is independently useful; enables coarse-to-fine retrieval (shortlist on short prefix, rerank with longer prefix). Reports up to 14× smaller embeddings at equal ImageNet-1K accuracy and up to 14× real-world retrieval speedups.
- **Binary/int8 + MRL**: "Binary and Scalar Embedding Quantization for Significantly Faster & Cheaper Retrieval" (Hugging Face + Mixedbread.ai blog, March 2024) combines MRL dimension truncation with per-dimension binary/int8 quantization; Mixedbread's own "64 bytes per embedding" post reports retaining 90.76% of original accuracy at 1.56% of original embedding size when slicing + 1-bit-quantizing. This is an engineering blog post, not a peer-reviewed paper — treat numbers as vendor-reported.
- **Matryoshka-Adaptor**: Unsupervised/supervised post-hoc tuning framework that further shrinks LLM embedding dimensionality (≈2× unsupervised, ≈6× supervised) without IR performance loss. arXiv:2407.20243.
- **Espresso / ESE**: "ESE: Espresso Sentence Embeddings" (formerly "2D Matryoshka Sentence Embeddings"), ICLR 2025. Adds a "learn-to-express" mechanism (depth-wise, weighting different transformer layers) on top of MRL's "learn-to-compress" (dimension-wise, using PCA to compact information into leading dimensions), enabling scaling in both depth and width. Code: github.com/SeanLee97/AnglE.
- **Relation to our direction**: MRL and its variants achieve variable-rate output codes by truncating along fixed, importance-ordered coordinate axes — the opposite design choice from "abandon the teacher's coordinate system." They have no threshold/top-k-specific loss term (the training objective is still a standard contrastive/InfoNCE loss applied redundantly across nested prefixes) and no false-positive guard. They are relevant as the standard baseline for "variable-rate embedding output" that any output-code scheme should be benchmarked against (as BitEmbed itself does — see §3.7, where BitEmbed reports it slightly beats MRL at most but not all storage budgets).

### 3.9 Asymmetric dense retrieval: KALE and related
- **KALE citation**: Campos, D. et al. "Quick Dense Retrievers Consume KALE: Post Training Kullback-Leibler Alignment of Embeddings for Asymmetrical Dual Encoders." SustaiNLP 2023 (ACL workshop). arXiv:2304.01016.
- **Mechanism**: Post-training layer-pruning of the *query* encoder (keeping the document encoder fixed/full-size), followed by KL-divergence alignment of the pruned query encoder's embedding distribution to the original (matching the frozen document encoder's space). Achieves asymmetric dual encoders where the query side is cheap and the doc side stays accurate.
- **Reported results**: 3× QPS with near-zero accuracy loss, 4.5× QPS with <2% loss; asymmetric+KALE model beats DistilBERT-based baselines despite 3× faster inference. Datasets: MS MARCO, Natural Questions, TriviaQA, SQuAD, SciFact.
- **Relation to our direction**: This is squarely "asymmetric query/document encoder" prior art (item 9 in the brief) but is about *size* asymmetry (small query encoder / big doc encoder), not *bit-width* asymmetry or threshold-aware quantization, and it explicitly aligns the pruned encoder's output distribution to the original teacher space (coordinate-preserving by design, the opposite of our direction).
- **Also found — closer to a "bit-asymmetric" framing**: **ASH** (Tepper & Willke, Intel, arXiv:2606.07870, 2026) — "Asymmetric Scalar Hashing With Learned Dimensionality Reduction for High-Fidelity Vector Quantization." Per the fetched summary: learned dimensionality reduction followed by scalar quantization, with **separate encoding precision for queries vs. documents** (queries higher precision, documents lower-bit, e.g. 4-bit/8-bit), and a reported "ranking-based objective to preserve top-k retrieval performance" rather than pure reconstruction, with the projection explicitly *not* preserving the original (teacher) coordinate system. This is one of the two or three closest single-paper analogues to the proposed direction found in this entire sweep — **it should be read in full before finalizing any novelty claim**, since the parts of the summary that most resemble our proposal (top-k-preserving ranking objective + coordinate-system abandonment + query/doc asymmetry) were produced by an automated PDF-summarization pass and need verification against the actual equations. Also related: "Asymmetric Hashing for Fast Ranking via Neural Network Measures" (Doan, Tan, Zhao, Li, SIGIR 2023, arXiv:2211.00619) — learns a pair of related hash functions mapping heterogeneous objects into a common discrete (binary) space via "discrete inner product fitting," for fast top-k ranking; exact loss and threshold-awareness not confirmed from the abstract alone.

### 3.10 Modern low-bit PTQ/QAT for encoders: BinaryBERT, TernaryBERT, I-BERT, ParetoQ, LLM-QAT, BitDistiller, EfficientQAT
- **TernaryBERT** (Zhang et al., EMNLP 2020, arXiv:2009.12812): ternarizes fine-tuned BERT weights via approximation-based and loss-aware ternarization with per-module granularity, plus knowledge distillation to offset accuracy loss; reports near-full-precision GLUE/SQuAD accuracy at 14.9× size reduction.
- **BinaryBERT** (Bai et al., ACL 2021, arXiv:2012.15701): pushes to 1-bit weights via "ternary weight splitting" (initialize a binary network by splitting a trained half-sized ternary network) to escape binary networks' harder loss landscape; 24× compression with modest GLUE/SQuAD degradation.
- **I-BERT** (Kim, Gholami, Yao, Mahoney, Keutzer, ICML 2021, arXiv:2101.01321): integer-only inference (INT8 weights and activations) using polynomial integer approximations of GELU/Softmax/LayerNorm; 2.4-4.0× speedup on T4 GPU, similar or slightly better accuracy than FP32.
- **LLM-QAT**: pioneering data-free QAT for LLMs via self-generated-data distillation matching teacher logits.
- **BitDistiller**: self-distillation with asymmetric clipping for low-bit LLM QAT.
- **EfficientQAT** (arXiv:2407.11062): two-phase QAT (block-wise full-parameter optimization, then quantization-parameter-only optimization) — e.g., 2-bit Llama-2-70B on one A100-80GB in 41 hours, <3-point degradation.
- **ParetoQ** (Meta, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2502.02631): first unified QAT framework spanning 1-, 1.58-, 2-, 3-, 4-bit in one comparable setup; finds a sharp learning-dynamics transition between 2-bit and 3-bit (below 3-bit, representations change drastically during QAT); ternary/2-bit/3-bit dominate the accuracy-size Pareto frontier over 4-bit and pure binary; a ternary 600M model reportedly beats a prior SOTA ternary 3B model.
- **Relation to our direction**: All are general-purpose LLM/encoder weight-quantization methods (classification/language-modeling accuracy objectives), not retrieval-functional or threshold/top-k objectives, and none touch output/index codes. They are the right citations for "state of the art in low-bit QAT mechanics" (ternary weight splitting, asymmetric clipping, staged QAT, scaling-law transitions at 2-3 bits) that a joint encoder+index method would build on, but none anticipate the retrieval-specific threshold framing.

### 3.11 Distillation objectives for embedding compression
- **Similarity-distribution distillation / Similarity Distribution Matching (SDM)**: matches student and teacher pairwise-similarity distributions over a memory queue via softmax + temperature (this is the same family of loss BitEmbed uses, §3.7); found referenced generically in embedding-distillation literature, not attributable to one canonical paper from this sweep.
- **Relational Knowledge Distillation (RKD)**: Park, W. et al., CVPR 2019, arXiv:1904.05068. Matches pairwise distance ("distance-wise") and triplet-angle ("angle-wise") relations between teacher and student embeddings rather than raw teacher outputs — a structure-preserving, not value-preserving, distillation loss, and one of the foundational "preserve relational geometry, not raw coordinates" ideas (relevant conceptual ancestor, even though it predates retrieval-specific threshold framing).
- **Jasper and Stella** (Dun Zhang et al., Dec 2024, arXiv:2412.19048): multi-teacher, multi-stage distillation into a 2B-parameter student ("Jasper"), using MRL for dimensionality reduction; reached #3 on the MTEB leaderboard (Dec 2024) with avg score 71.54/56 datasets. Distillation losses are described as standard multi-teacher alignment losses (not threshold/top-k-specific per the retrieved summary).
- **No paper found in this sweep that explicitly replaces full-distribution similarity-KL with a threshold- or top-k-restricted KD objective for embeddings.** This absence is itself a data point for the novelty argument (see §4).

### 3.12 Learned/optimized binary/scalar codes for a fixed embedding space
- **ITQ — Iterative Quantization** (Gong, Lazebnik et al., TPAMI 2013; foundational, pre-dating the 2020-2026 window but the base case every later "code-only" method compares to): PCA dimensionality reduction + an orthogonal (Procrustean) rotation minimizing binary quantization error, provably preserving pairwise distances better than random rotation. Code: github.com/TreezzZ/ITQ_PyTorch (reimplementation).
- **RaBitQ** (Gao & Long, SIGMOD 2024): randomized-rotation 1-bit-per-dimension quantization with a *proven theoretical error bound* on distance estimation (not a ranking/threshold loss — a worst-case-error guarantee); unbiased distance estimator, SIMD/bitwise-friendly. Outperforms PQ variants on accuracy-efficiency tradeoff. Code: github.com/VectorDB-NTU/RaBitQ-Library.
- **Extended RaBitQ** (SIGMOD 2025, arXiv:2409.09913): generalizes to arbitrary per-dimension bit-widths (3,4,5,7,8,9-bit variants shown), "asymptotically optimal" quantization; biggest gains reported in the 2-to-6-bit range. Same repo.
- **BBQ — Better Binary Quantization** (Elastic, Lucene/Elasticsearch, derived from RaBitQ ideas, GA default since Elasticsearch 9.1, July 2025): 1 bit/dim plus oversample-and-rerank two-stage search; ~95% memory reduction, 20-30× faster index build and 2-5× faster query vs. PQ, "no additional loss in accuracy" (vendor claim).
- **LVQ — Locally-Adaptive (Vector) Quantization** (Intel, VLDB 2023 + "Locally-Adaptive Quantization for Streaming Vector Search," arXiv:2402.02044, 2024): per-vector adaptive scalar quantization (each vector gets its own scale/shift), designed for graph-based ANN with SIMD-friendly distance computation; Turbo-LVQ and multi-means LVQ extensions report up to 27-28% further search-performance gains. Code: github.com/intel/ScalableVectorSearch.
- **Relation to our direction**: This entire family optimizes codes for **uniform distance-estimation fidelity or provable worst-case error bounds**, not for a **relevance threshold or top-k neighborhood specifically** — RaBitQ/Extended RaBitQ/LVQ/BBQ are "how accurately can I estimate any distance," which is a strictly harder and more general goal than "preserve which docs cross a threshold, and their order." None reported using an asymmetric false-positive guard or being evaluated on threshold-crossing preservation specifically (their target metric is recall@k against exact brute-force k-NN, which is related to but not identical to "which docs cross a teacher relevance threshold").

### 3.13 Rate-constrained / joint encoder+index optimization
Covered primarily in the table (§2) and §3.2. Summary of the family not already discussed: **LibVQ** (github.com/staoxiao/LibVQ) is presented as "the first library for dense-retrieval-oriented vector quantization," bundling JPQ-, Distill-VQ-, and RepCONC-style joint training under one interface, with the explicit finding (per search summary) that ranking/relevance-loss-based joint methods (JPQ, RepCONC, MoPQ) outperform reconstruction-loss-based joint methods (Poeem). This finding is itself indirect supporting evidence for the premise of the proposed research direction — that a task-aligned (not reconstruction) loss is what makes joint encoder+index training pay off — but none of these libraries touch encoder weight quantization (they all assume an fp32 encoder) and none define a threshold/superlevel-set-specific objective.

Also newly surfaced in 2026: differentiable/end-to-end learnable ANN indexing continues as an active line (e.g., UniSearch, ETEGRec-style methods using Straight-Through Estimators or Gumbel-Softmax relaxation to bypass non-differentiable quantization steps for generative/learned-index retrieval), per a 2026 survey-style search snippet — these are largely in the generative-retrieval / learned-index space rather than classic PQ/ANN, and none were confirmed to include a threshold-aware objective.

### 3.14 Threshold-aware / boundary-aware / superlevel-set framing — the direct novelty check
This is the most important search for the "is this novel" question. Findings, in decreasing order of relevance:
- **"On the Theoretical Limitations of Embedding-Based Retrieval"** (Weller, Boratko, Naim, Lee — Google DeepMind & Johns Hopkins, arXiv:2508.21038, Aug 2025): Not a quantization paper. Proves via sign-rank/communication-complexity arguments that for any fixed embedding dimension d, there exist combinatorial sets of top-k document subsets that **no single-vector dot-product embedding model can realize as the top-k result for any query**, and introduces the LIMIT stress-test dataset showing even SOTA models fail on simple instances of this. **Relevance**: this is a hard theoretical ceiling on how well *any* fixed-dimension embedding (quantized or not) can preserve arbitrary top-k/superlevel structure — worth citing as a caveat/bound on what a threshold-preserving output code can promise, especially at very low output bit-budgets where effective capacity shrinks further.
- **"Boundary-Aware Quantization: Finite-Scale Decision Geometry of Neural Classifiers"** (arXiv:2607.01478, July 2026): Found, but **about classifier decision boundaries, not retrieval/embedding threshold-crossing**. It defines a "boundary-aware profile" for quantization that additionally constrains local displacement, normal stability, and boundary Jaccard distance for a classifier's decision surface, choosing bit-width based on a boundary-preservation stopping criterion rather than plain accuracy. Structurally analogous vocabulary ("boundary-aware," "decision geometry") to what a retrieval threshold-crossing paper would need, but it is not about retrieval, similarity thresholds, or embedding codes — a useful cross-domain technique/vocabulary reference, not a competitor.
- **CQ-CiM** (arXiv:2602.20083, DAC 2026) and **ASH** (arXiv:2606.07870, 2026) — see §3.9 and the table — are the two works whose *reported* objectives (per fetched summaries) most resemble "preserve ranking/top-k under quantization" rather than reconstruct values. Neither, per the retrieved summaries, frames the loss explicitly as **superlevel-set preservation of a relevance threshold** or includes an explicit **asymmetric false-positive guard** distinct from a general ranking-preservation term; both need a full read before ruling in or out.
- **No hits** for "superlevel set," "decision-boundary preserving distillation embedding," "hard-negative guard quantization," or "false-positive-aware hashing" as a named technique in the retrieval-quantization literature. Searches for these exact/near-exact terms returned either unrelated papers or generic hard-negative-mining papers (about *data selection* for contrastive training, not about *quantization-induced* false positives near a decision boundary).
- **Deep Cauchy Hashing** (§3.3) remains the closest *named, published, threshold-explicit* loss function in the whole sweep (Hamming-radius threshold in the loss), but it is 2018, image-only, symmetric (not an asymmetric false-positive-only guard), and not about a jointly-trained low-bit encoder.
- **Verdict**: We found no paper that combines (a) an explicit relevance-threshold/superlevel-set framing, (b) an asymmetric one-sided false-positive-guard loss (as distinct from ScaNN-style continuous-magnitude weighting or DCH-style symmetric radius weighting), and (c) joint quantization of encoder weights and output index codes, for text/multilingual retrieval. Components (a)+(b) appear nowhere combined; (c) exists (JPQ/Distill-VQ/RepCONC) but always with an fp32 encoder and a continuous ranking/KD loss, never a binary threshold-crossing loss. This supports treating the full combination as a novel synthesis, while requiring explicit differentiation from ScaNN, DCH, JPQ/Distill-VQ, and BitEmbed in any paper.

### 3.15 Vocabulary / embedding table compression for multilingual encoders
- **Ushio, Zhou, Camacho-Collados**, "An Efficient Multilingual Language Model Compression through Vocabulary Trimming (VT)," EMNLP 2023 Findings, arXiv:2305.15020. Removes vocabulary tokens irrelevant to a target language from a multilingual LM's embedding table, converting a multilingual model into an effectively monolingual one without retraining. Reported: 35% compression is "sufficient to keep the original performance" of mT5 on QA/QG, ~39% for XLM-R on NLI, ~55% on sentiment analysis — compression ratios vary substantially by task/model.
- **Post-Training 4-bit Quantization on Embedding Tables** (arXiv:1911.02079, pre-dates the window but foundational): row-wise/4-bit PTQ for large (recommendation-style) embedding tables.
- **General embedding-table compression toolbox** found via search: frequency-based vocabulary pruning (keep top-K most frequent tokens, as in OpenBA-V2), low-rank/SVD factorization of the embedding matrix (qd → (q+d)r parameters), and generic PTQ INT4/INT8 on embedding tables — these are standard, not novel, techniques; no 2025-2026 paper specifically targeting *multilingual retrieval* vocabulary+quantization jointly was found beyond Ushio et al. (2023, monolingual-target trimming, not a retrieval-functional threshold objective) and the general "On Multilingual Encoder Language Model Compression for Low-Resource Languages" survey-style paper (arXiv:2505.16956, not independently read in depth here).
- **Relation to our direction**: Vocabulary trimming is a real, cheap lever "H5" (per the project's own plan.md) but is orthogonal to the threshold/top-k quantization question — no evidence found that anyone has combined vocabulary trimming with a relevance-threshold-preserving joint QAT objective for multilingual retrieval encoders.

### 3.16 SPLADE / late-interaction binary variants
Search coverage here was comparatively thin. Generic sources (Qdrant, Pinecone, Zilliz educational blogs) describe SPLADE as a learned sparse model typically compared to BM25 or dense hybrid pipelines, and note that "binary quantization is generally more effective for high-dimensional [dense] vectors" as a passing remark, but **no paper was found that specifically benchmarks a binary/low-bit dense-vector scheme against a binary/quantized SPLADE or late-interaction (ColBERT-style) variant as a head-to-head comparator for the threshold/top-k-preservation question.** Token-pooling for multi-vector retrieval (arXiv:2409.14683, "Reducing the Footprint of Multi-Vector Retrieval... via Token Pooling") and WARP (arXiv:2501.17788, efficient multi-vector engine) are adjacent efficiency papers for late-interaction models but are about pooling/engine efficiency, not bit-width quantization with a threshold objective. **Treat this area as under-covered in this sweep** — if late-interaction/SPLADE comparisons matter to the program, a dedicated follow-up search is recommended.

## 4. Claims we must NOT make / claims that appear defensible

### Claims we must NOT make (as of 2026-09-02)
1. **"No prior work optimizes retrieval-index codes for anything other than reconstruction error."** — False; ScaNN's anisotropic loss, JPQ/Distill-VQ/RepCONC/MoPQ/Poeem's ranking/KD losses, RaBitQ's bounded-error estimator, and Deep Cauchy Hashing's threshold-radius loss are all non-reconstruction objectives already in wide use (§3.1, §3.2, §3.3, §3.12).
2. **"No prior work jointly trains an encoder and its output/index codes."** — False; JPQ, Distill-VQ, RepCONC, Poeem, MoPQ are all explicitly joint-training methods (§3.2, §3.13), and BitEmbed jointly trains a ternary encoder with a multi-precision output head (§3.7), even though none of these match every feature of our proposal.
3. **"No prior work uses an asymmetric penalty in a hashing/quantization loss."** — False; ScaNN's parallel/orthogonal asymmetry and Deep Cauchy Hashing's threshold-radius weighting are both asymmetric with respect to some structural axis, even though neither is asymmetric with respect to *false positives specifically*, one-sided at a *relevance threshold*.
4. **"BitEmbed doesn't address output quantization."** — False; it explicitly supports and trains for 1/2/4/8/16-bit output embeddings (§3.7) — any positioning must engage with this directly, not claim BitEmbed is "just" a weight-quantization paper.
5. **"No prior theoretical work addresses limits of top-k preservation under compression."** — False; Weller et al. 2025 (arXiv:2508.21038) proves fundamental top-k-realizability limits tied to embedding dimension, independent of quantization (§3.14) — any claim that a threshold/top-k-preserving scheme can match full-precision top-k arbitrarily well at arbitrarily low dimension/bit-budget would contradict this result and must be scoped carefully (e.g., "preserves top-k as well as the *unquantized* embedding of the same dimension can," not "preserves top-k absolutely").
6. **"Deep hashing has never used a threshold in its loss."** — False; Deep Cauchy Hashing (2018) explicitly penalizes similar pairs exceeding a Hamming-distance threshold (§3.3, §3.14).
7. **Do not claim REDH's exact identity or method** — it could not be verified in this sweep (§3.4); do not cite it as a specific prior-art precedent without first locating its original paper.
8. **Do not claim priority over ASH (arXiv:2606.07870) or CQ-CiM (arXiv:2602.20083) without reading their full text.** Per fetched (automated) summaries, both may already claim top-k/ranking-preservation objectives and (for ASH) explicit abandonment of the teacher coordinate system with query/doc-asymmetric bit-widths — if those summaries are accurate on a full read, ASH in particular could substantially narrow the novelty claim for the "index code" half of the proposal (though it does not touch weight quantization or joint encoder training, and was not confirmed to include an explicit asymmetric *false-positive* guard or a formal threshold/superlevel-set formulation).

### Claims that appear defensible as of 2026-09
1. **"No published work combines joint ternary-weight encoder quantization with a threshold-crossing/superlevel-set-preserving output-code objective and an explicit asymmetric false-positive guard, for text retrieval."** Closest works: BitEmbed (§3.7) has the joint ternary-encoder + multi-bit-output half but uses full-distribution similarity KL, not a threshold/top-k-restricted loss, and preserves teacher coordinates; JPQ/Distill-VQ/RepCONC (§3.2, §3.13) have the joint-training + non-reconstruction-loss half but use fp32 encoders and continuous ranking/KD losses, not threshold-crossing losses.
2. **"No published retrieval-quantization loss is explicitly framed around preserving a superlevel set of a fixed (or student-calibrated) relevance threshold, as distinct from preserving the full similarity ranking or the full similarity distribution."** Closest: Distill-VQ's "reproduce teacher relevance predictions" (soft, full-distribution) and BitEmbed's similarity-KL (soft, full-distribution) both differ from a hard threshold/superlevel-set target; Deep Cauchy Hashing's Hamming-radius threshold (§3.3) is the closest but is a *fixed geometric radius in Hamming space* for images, not a *relevance-threshold-in-teacher-cosine-space* concept transferred to text retrieval with an asymmetric guard.
3. **"No published work explicitly argues for deliberately discarding the teacher's coordinate system as a design principle for the below-threshold region, while preserving decisions above it."** Everything found either preserves the teacher's coordinates by construction (MRL/Matryoshka family, KALE, BitEmbed's similarity distillation) or optimizes a coordinate-free index metric uniformly across the whole space regardless of relevance (RaBitQ/LVQ/BBQ's uniform distance-estimation fidelity) — nobody found combines "coordinate-free below threshold" with "decision-preserving above/near threshold" as a stated, deliberate asymmetry. (Caveat: ASH's summary suggests something adjacent — verify before leaning on this claim too heavily.)
4. **"An explicit, named, one-sided false-positive guard for hard negatives just below a relevance threshold — distinct from ScaNN's magnitude-based asymmetry and DCH's symmetric radius weighting — does not appear in the reviewed 2018-2026 hashing/quantization/distillation literature."** This is the most defensible specific claim, conditional on ASH's and CQ-CiM's full text not contradicting it (§3.9, §3.14).

## 5. Practical facts for engineering

**BitNet embedding models (Microsoft)**
- HF model ids: `microsoft/bitnet-embedding-270m` and `microsoft/bitnet-embedding-0.6b`. Both MIT licensed.
- `bitnet-embedding-0.6b`: decoder-only, Qwen3-0.6B backbone converted to BitNet (ternary linear layers + SubLN), 28 layers, hidden size 1024, 16 attention heads / 8 KV heads (GQA), head dim 128, vocab 151,936 (Qwen3 tokenizer), output embedding dim 1024, last-token (EOS) pooling + L2 normalize, 94 supported languages.
- `bitnet-embedding-270m`: decoder-only, Gemma3-270M backbone converted to BitNet, 18 layers, 4 attention heads / 1 KV head (GQA), head dim 256, intermediate size 2048, GELU, Gemma tokenizer (262,144 vocab), 32,768 context length, output embedding dim 640, last-token pooling + L2 normalize, supports ~41 listed languages (broad European + several Asian/Middle Eastern languages listed on the card).
- Weight/activation quantization: ternary {−Δ,0,+Δ} absmean-quantized weights (1.58-bit) in all linear layers, 8-bit per-token activations (W1.58A8).
- Output embedding quantization: selectable at 1-bit (sign + absmax scale), 2/4/8-bit (uniform scalar with absmax scale), or 16-bit (fp16, unquantized) — a single checkpoint supports all five via the paper's Stage-3 multi-precision training.
- Runtime: both model cards report **bitnet.cpp support** (the dedicated framework at github.com/microsoft/BitNet) and **llama.cpp support via GGUF/BF16**. bitnet.cpp is a CPU-first inference engine; the model cards' reported CPU throughput speedups (2.06× for the 0.6B, 1.74× for the 270M, vs. FP16 baseline) were measured with 8 CPU threads.
- Paper: arXiv:2606.25674, "BitNet Text Embeddings," submitted 24 Jun 2026 (revised 22 Jul 2026), authors Li, Huang, Wang, Yang, Song, Xia, Wu, Huang, Zhang, Wei, Zhao.
- Recommended benchmark for apples-to-apples comparison: **MMTEB (English, v2)** and its multilingual variant, both used by BitEmbed itself — reported per-category scores are Bitext Mining, Classification, Clustering, Pair Classification, Reranking, **Retrieval**, and STS, with an overall mean. The paper explicitly flags **Retrieval as the most quantization-sensitive category** (largest point-drops across the 1/2/4/8/16-bit output sweep) — this is the category to prioritize for any comparison against a proposed threshold-aware scheme. Standard BEIR subsets (e.g., SciFact, NFCorpus, FiQA, ArguAna, TREC-COVID — exactly the ones already listed in this project's own `research/plan.md` dataset plan) are the natural drill-down since MMTEB's Retrieval category is itself BEIR-derived.
- No separate training-code repository was located for BitEmbed; only the two HF model weight repos and the general `microsoft/BitNet` inference-framework repo.

### 7.1 ASH — arXiv:2606.07870v2 ("ASH: Asymmetric Scalar Hashing With Learned Dimensionality Reduction for High-Fidelity Vector Quantization")

**ID check**: Resolves correctly. Authors are **Mariano Tepper (Elastic)** and **Theodore Willke (IBM WatsonX)** — note this **corrects** the affiliation given in §2/§3.9/§6 of this file, which listed both authors as "Intel, 2026." The paper itself lists Elastic and IBM WatsonX, not Intel, as of this submission (v2, 24 Aug 2026). This is a factual correction, not a retraction of the paper's identity — title/topic/authors otherwise match what was assumed in the earlier sweep.

**1. Exact objective function.** ASH learns a projection matrix **W** (and per-vector codes {v_i}) by solving, verbatim (Eq. 5):

> min over {v_i ∈ V_b^d}, W ∈ St(d,D) of: (1/2) · Σ_i ‖z_i − ‖v_i‖₂⁻¹ · Wᵀv_i‖₂²

"that minimizes the average reconstruction error." (§3, immediately after Eq. 5). This is a pure **angular reconstruction-error** loss (the paper states: "By construction, the reconstruction error in our architecture is purely angular, in contrast to other quantizers that carry angular and norm errors"). There is no second loss term, no ranking term, no contrastive term anywhere in the paper. St(d,D) is the Stiefel manifold (row-orthonormal d×D matrices); V_b is the scalar codebook {2c−2^b+1 | c=0..2^b−1} for bitrate b per dimension.

**2. Scope / asymmetric FP-vs-FN penalty.** The loss sums over **all** indexed vectors uniformly (Eq. 5 is an unweighted average over the full dataset {z_i}). There is **no threshold, no top-k restriction, and no neighborhood restriction** in the training objective, and **no asymmetric false-positive/false-negative term of any kind**. The word "asymmetric" in the title/paper refers exclusively to **query-vs-document representation asymmetry** (query stays full-precision float; only the document/database vector is quantized — Eq. 2, "the query retains its original form and only the indexed vector is quantized"), a decades-old idea inherited directly from Product Quantization (Jégou et al. 2010, cited explicitly as introducing this asymmetric-similarity paradigm in the 2010s). ASH's own related-work section is explicit that its contribution is *learning the projection+quantizer from data*, not a new loss shape: "we show that data-driven asymmetric scalar quantization combines the best of both worlds" — data-driven meets an existing (not new) asymmetric-computation paradigm.

**3. Coordinates vs. orderings.** ASH explicitly targets reproducing the vector's **actual coordinates** (post dimensionality-reduction), not just orderings or top-k sets. The encoder/decoder pair is defined as g(z;W) := quant_b(Wz), f(v;W) := ‖v‖₂⁻¹Wᵀv, with the stated goal z ≈ f(g(z;W);W) — i.e., reconstruct z itself. The similarity approximation used at query time, ⟨q,x⟩ ≈ ⟨q, quant(x)⟩ (Eq. 2), is also a **value-approximation** framing (approximate the true inner product value), not a decision/ranking framing. Search-time evaluation is via **10-recall@R** (does the approximate top-R match brute-force exact top-10) — a standard ANN recall metric computed post hoc, not any part of the training loss. **Coordinate-free framing is explicitly false for this paper** — it corrects the "reportedly yes... coordinate-free" entry in §2/§3.9 of this file, which was appropriately hedged ("verify before finalizing") and should now be read as ruled out.

**4. Encoder jointly quantized?** No. ASH is a **pure index/post-hoc quantizer**: "the encoder g reduces the dimensionality from D to d dimensions and performs quantization while the decoder f expands it back" — g/f operate on an already-computed embedding z ∈ S^(D−1); no text/image encoder is trained, fine-tuned, or touched. Input embeddings (e.g., OpenAI, Cohere) are treated as fixed, black-box, frozen vectors to be indexed.

**5. Bit-widths.** Output codes: variable bitrate b per dimension (paper works out b=1 and b=2 in the main experiments, e.g. Table 1 payload formula d = ⌊(B − 2·16 − ⌈log₂C⌉)/b⌋ for a B-bit total budget; b=1 recovers the classical ITQ algorithm exactly, shown in Eq. 26). Payload per vector also includes a 16-bit (FP16/BF16) scale, a 16-bit offset, and ⌈log₂C⌉ bits for a landmark/cluster index. **Query side: full float32, unquantized, no bit-width reduction at all** — this is not "4-bit/8-bit asymmetric," as the earlier §2/§3.9 table entries stated; it is "unquantized query vs. b-bit document," the same asymmetry PQ has used since 2010. Weights (the learned projection matrix W) also remain full-precision float; nothing about ASH quantizes network/projection *weights* — only the output vector codes are quantized.

**6. Domain / datasets / results.** Domain: generic ANN vector search over off-the-shelf text embeddings (no query-relevance labels/qrels used; this is a "recall vs. exact brute-force k-NN" benchmark, not an IR/BEIR-style relevance benchmark). Datasets (Table 5, confirmed from text): `openai-1536-100k`, `openai-3072-100k`, `cohere-1m`, `openai-1536-1m`, `openai-3072-1m`, `openai-v3-large-1536-100k`, `openai-v3-large-3072-100k`, `dbpedia-entities-openai3-text-embedding-3-large-{1536,3072}-1m` — i.e., pre-computed OpenAI/Cohere embedding dumps, evaluated as generic high-dimensional vector sets. No "teacher model" in the distillation sense — the "ground truth" is exact brute-force nearest-neighbor search on the same embeddings. Headline claim (verbatim): "ASH achieves state-of-the-art ANN recall across all compression regimes, outperforming alternatives by a wide margin. In several cases, ASH with half the total bits matches or exceeds the alternatives' accuracy" — compared against PQ, LOPQ, EDEN, RaBitQ/Extended RaBitQ, and TurboQuant, measured as 10-recall@R and QPS/latency (Table 3: ASH masked-load latency 8 cycles / throughput 0.5 vs. PQ gather latency 30 cycles / throughput 9.75).

**7. Guard band?** None found. No margin, uncertainty band, or boundary-region concept anywhere in the paper — searched explicitly for "margin," "guard," "band," "uncertain," "false positive/negative," "hard negative"; the only hit for "margin" is the informal phrase "outperforming alternatives by a wide margin" in the conclusion, unrelated to a decision-boundary margin.

**8. Code available?** Yes — explicitly linked in the paper: **github.com/marianotepper/ash** and **github.com/tlwillke/ivf-ash-fastscan** (both verified reachable, HTTP 200, as of 2026-09-02).

**Verdict — does ASH cover "joint rate-constrained quantization of encoder AND index using threshold-crossing risk + local ranking as the functional objective, without preserving the teacher coordinate system"?**
**No.** ASH (a) never touches an encoder (pure post-hoc index quantizer on frozen embeddings), (b) uses a pure angular-reconstruction-error loss over all vectors uniformly — no threshold, no top-k/ranking term, no asymmetric FP/FN penalty, (c) explicitly targets coordinate reconstruction, the opposite of "coordinate-free." Its only real overlap with the target claim is the classical query-full-precision/document-quantized asymmetry inherited from PQ (2010), which is not novel to ASH and is not the same axis of asymmetry as "asymmetric bit-widths chosen by relevance-threshold risk." This is a materially stronger (more negative) finding than the earlier "reportedly yes, verify before relying on this" hedge in §2/§3.9/§4 — those hedges should now be resolved in the "ruled out" direction.

### 7.2 CQ-CiM — arXiv:2602.20083v3 ("CQ-CiM: Hardware-Aware Embedding Shaping for Robust CiM-Based Retrieval")

**ID check**: Resolves correctly, DAC 2026 (conference field literally still shows the submission-template placeholder "Make sure to enter the correct conference title..." in the HTML — a proof draft artifact, not a content issue). Authors: Xinzhao Li, Alptekin Vardar, Franz Müller, Navya Goli, Umamaheswara Rao Tida, Kai Ni, Xiaobo Sharon Hu, Thomas Kämpfe, Ruiyang Qin (Villanova, Fraunhofer IPMS, NDSU, Notre Dame, TU Braunschweig).

**1. Exact objective function.** Three named losses, verbatim (Eqs. 3-5):

> Contrastive: L_CSE = −log[ e^(sim(h_i,h_i⁺)/τ) / Σ_{j=1}^{N} (e^(sim(h_i,h_j⁺)/τ) + e^(sim(h_i,h_j⁻)/τ)) ]
> Reconstruction: L_MSE = ‖f_orig(x) − f̂_cq(x)‖₂²
> Overall: L = L_CSE + λ_MSE · L_MSE, with λ_MSE ≈ 5–10 for 384→128 compression.

L_CSE is SimCSE-style (Gao et al. 2021): two dropout-masked views of the same sentence form a positive pair, all other in-batch samples are negatives — a completely standard InfoNCE/contrastive loss, used "primarily to update the LoRA adapter." L_MSE is a **full-vector, all-dimensions** reconstruction loss between the original float embedding and the final compressed+quantized embedding.

**2. Scope / asymmetric FP-vs-FN penalty.** L_CSE acts on **all in-batch pairs** (every other sample is a negative, weighted identically by the softmax). L_MSE acts on **all embedding dimensions uniformly** ("minimizes the distance between the original floating-point embedding and the final compressed-quantized output" — no per-dimension or per-pair weighting scheme). **No threshold, no top-k restriction, no explicit asymmetric false-positive/false-negative term** anywhere in §3.4 or elsewhere in the paper.

**3. Coordinates vs. orderings.** Mixed/soft: the MSE term explicitly targets **reproducing the original embedding's coordinate values** ("the distance between the original floating-point embedding and the final compressed-quantized output"); the contrastive term targets **relative in-batch similarity structure** (SimCSE-style), not raw coordinates. Neither term is restricted to a decision threshold or top-k set — both act on the full value/full-batch level. This is the same two-term family (contrastive + value-reconstruction) as several already-catalogued works (Distill-VQ's soft relevance-matching, BitEmbed's contrastive+distillation, MoPQ's contrastive), not a threshold/superlevel-set-specific design.

**4. Encoder jointly quantized/trained?** **Yes — and this corrects an error in §2 of this file.** The existing table entry for CQ-CiM says "No (targets a hardware deployment layer on top of an existing embedding)" for "Joint encoder+index?" — this is **wrong**. CQ-CiM explicitly fine-tunes the backbone sentence-embedding encoder end-to-end via a **LoRA adapter**: "LoRA injects a lightweight low-rank update into selected weight matrices, allowing the encoder to adjust its representations... This enables the embedding model to adapt to our downstream compression and quantization objectives." The paper explicitly rejected both freezing the encoder ("limits the framework's learning space") and full fine-tuning ("risks creating a parameter imbalance") in favor of LoRA as a middle ground (§3.2). So: **the encoder is adapted jointly with the compression+quantization heads, end-to-end, in one training loop** — this is much closer to "joint encoder+index training" than the earlier sweep credited it with. Important nuance: LoRA adapts the encoder's *representations*, but the base encoder weights (and the LoRA update itself) remain in **full floating-point precision** — there is no ternary/low-bit weight quantization of the encoder itself; only the final **output embedding** is bit-quantized (via N2UQ). So it is joint-training-of-encoder-and-codes, but not joint-*bit-quantization*-of-encoder-weights-and-codes.

**5. Bit-widths.** Output embedding: evaluated at **1-bit, 1.58-bit, and 2-bit** (2-bit is called out as the realistic CiM target: "FeFET-based CiM arrays realistically support around 2 stable levels"), using the **N2UQ (Nonuniform-to-Uniform Quantization)** method (Gholami et al. 2021), originally designed for *model-weight* quantization but repurposed here for embedding-output quantization. Output dimension: 384 → 128 (compression head). No weight/activation bit-width is specified for the LoRA-adapted encoder itself (it is not quantized — see point 4). **No query/doc asymmetry**: confirmed by direct search — the deployment description states the CQ-CiM block "shapes the corpus **and query** embeddings before they are sent to the CiM crossbar," i.e., the identical pipeline (same LoRA+compression+quantization heads) is applied to both queries and documents; no differential precision is described anywhere in the paper.

**6. Domain / datasets / results.** Domain: text retrieval for edge/on-device RAG, explicitly targeting Compute-in-Memory hardware (SRAM, ReRAM, FeFET crossbar arrays; Table 1 gives measured device-variation profiles for RRAM₁, FeFET₂, FeFET₃, RRAM₄, FeFET₆ with 1 or 4 conductance levels and Gaussian noise σ_v = 0.0026–0.0155). Base embedding model: `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`. Five retrieval datasets: ARCChallenge (9.35k docs), NanoHotpotQA (5.09k), CQADupStack (37.6k), FiQA (57.6k), ArguAna (8.67k) — note the paper's own intro paragraph (§4.1) lists only "ARCChallenge, NanoHotpotQA, CQADupStackGisRetrieval, and ArguAna" (FiQA is omitted from that sentence despite being a column in Table 2 — an internal inconsistency in the paper itself, flagged here rather than silently reconciled). Baselines: UMAP, PCA+uniform quantization, "Vanilla" (native-scale truncation), plus PQ-8bit as a non-CiM-compatible high-precision reference. Headline result (Table 2, 2-bit, ArguAna): CQ-CiM Recall@5 = 0.529 / nDCG@10 = 0.433, vs. Vanilla 0.468/0.385, PCA 0.410/0.338, UMAP 0.230/0.190; CQ-CiM is reported as best-or-most-competitive across all 5 datasets at 1-bit, 1.58-bit, and 2-bit. End-to-end RAG evaluation under 5 simulated device-variation profiles (D-1 to D-5) shows "modest degradation under device noise" (Fig. 6). No teacher model in a distillation sense — this is self-supervised (dropout-based contrastive) fine-tuning against the model's own float output, not KD from a separate larger teacher.

**7. Guard band?** None found. Noise injection (Eqs. 1-2, a level-dependent Gaussian noise mask added to the compressed embedding before quantization, calibrated from measured device variance) simulates hardware unreliability but is not framed as a decision-boundary margin or uncertain-region concept — it's a robustness/data-augmentation mechanism, not a threshold-crossing guard band.

**8. Code available?** **No.** No GitHub link or other code repository is given anywhere in the paper; the only GitHub reference on the page is arXiv's own generic "Report GitHub Issue" UI widget, unrelated to the paper's own code.

**Verdict — does CQ-CiM cover "joint rate-constrained quantization of encoder AND index using threshold-crossing risk + local ranking as the functional objective, without preserving the teacher coordinate system"?**
**Partially — the "joint encoder+index" half is real (revise §2's table), but the functional-objective half is not.** CQ-CiM does jointly train an encoder (via LoRA) together with compression and quantization heads in one end-to-end loop, which is a genuine correction upward from the earlier table's "No." However, its loss is a standard SimCSE contrastive term plus a full-vector MSE reconstruction term — there is no threshold-crossing risk, no top-k/local-ranking-restricted term, and no asymmetric false-positive guard; the MSE term explicitly preserves the original embedding's coordinate values (the opposite of "coordinate-free"), and the encoder's own weights are never bit-quantized (only the final embedding output is). Net: overlaps on "joint training loop touching the encoder," diverges on every other element of the target claim (objective shape, threshold/top-k restriction, asymmetric FP guard, coordinate-freedom, weight quantization).

### 7.3 Weller et al. 2025 — arXiv:2508.21038 ("On the Theoretical Limitations of Embedding-Based Retrieval")

**Exact theorem statement (Theorem 1, "Dimension lower bound," §3).** Setup: unit document vectors v_1,...,v_n ∈ ℝ^d, unit query vectors u ∈ ℝ^d. A k-subset S ⊆ [n] is "realized with margin γ" if there exists a unit query u_S such that (Eq. 1, verbatim):

> min_{i∈S} ⟨u_S, v_i⟩ ≥ max_{j∉S} ⟨u_S, v_j⟩ + 2γ

Theorem: "Assume 1 ≤ k < n and that every k-subset S ⊆ [n] is realized with margin γ as in equation 1. Then" (Eq. 2, verbatim):

> C(n,k) ≤ (1 + 1/γ)^d, hence d ≥ log[C(n,k)] / log(1 + 1/γ)

(C(n,k) = binomial coefficient "n choose k"). Proof is a sphere-packing argument: any two distinct realized k-subsets require unit queries u_S, u_T that are pairwise ≥2γ-separated, so C(n,k) disjoint γ-radius balls must pack inside a ball of radius 1+γ in ℝ^d, giving the volume inequality M·γ^d ≤ (1+γ)^d.

**Asymptotic/numeric form.** For n ≫ k, log C(n,k) ≈ k·log(en/k), so the bound becomes **d = Ω(k·log(en/k) / log(1+1/γ))** (stated explicitly as this asymptotic formula in §3.0.1). At γ=0.1 (the paper's "approximately standard" empirical value): d ≥ ⌈log C(n,k) / log 11⌉. Table 1 gives concrete numbers: e.g. n=10⁶, k=10 → d≥52; n=10⁶, k=100 → d≥425; n=10⁸, k=1000 → d≥5217; n=10²,k=1000 is "trivial" (only one k-subset exists when k=n). The paper notes explicitly: "a stricter margin requirement (larger γ) demands higher dimension, since log(1+1/γ) decreases with γ" — i.e., **more separation margin costs more dimension**, monotonically.

**Binary/short-code (64-512 bit) regime — direct textual coverage is thin; treat the following as scoped, not overclaimed.** The paper's theorem is about **real-valued embedding dimension d**, not bit-width per coordinate — it does not derive a separate bound as a function of bits-per-dimension or total bit budget, and does not analyze 64-512-bit binary codes as a distinct regime. The only direct textual bridge to quantization is: "most embeddings used for web-scale search are quantized or truncated (e.g. through Matryoshka embeddings) to less than 1k dimensions... We see that even with a moderate margin, **which is needed to handle noise from messy data or quantization**, the lower bounds in Table 1 can already be larger than what is used in practice." I.e., the paper's own framing treats quantization purely as a **source of effective-margin inflation** (quantization noise forces a larger γ to keep decisions robust) rather than as a separate dimension-count variable — and since the bound shows *larger* γ requires *larger*, not smaller, d, this reads as "quantization makes the existing dimension bound harder to satisfy, not easier," but the paper never works this out as an explicit formula for a target bit-budget. **Any claim in our project's materials that Weller et al. "bounds very short binary codes (64-512 bits)" specifically must be phrased as our own extrapolation** (quantizing a d-real-dimension embedding to b bits/dim to fit a 64-512-bit budget can only shrink the number of realizable k-subsets further, and effectively demands a larger margin per the sphere-packing argument, both of which push a fixed (n,k) target further out of reach) **and not attributed to the paper as a stated result.**

**Related appendix result worth citing precisely.** In the appendix, the paper defines "row-wise order-preserving rank" (rank_rop) and "row-wise thresholdable rank" (rank_rt) for a relevance matrix, and proves (paraphrasing the identity, quoting the key line): "For binary matrices, row-wise ordering/thresholding are equivalent notions of representation capacity" and formally, "For a binary matrix A ∈ {0,1}^(m×n), we have that rank_rop(A) = rank_rt(A)." This is directly relevant to the target research question: Weller et al. themselves prove that, **for a binary relevance/qrel matrix, "preserve the ranking" and "preserve the threshold-crossing decision" require exactly the same representational capacity** — i.e., the distinction the target research direction wants to draw (threshold-crossing vs. full ranking as the functional target) collapses to the same theoretical quantity in Weller's framework when relevance is binary. This should be read as a **supporting theoretical citation** for treating threshold-preservation and top-k/ranking-preservation as the same design target, not as a competing result.

**LIMIT dataset and empirical results.** LIMIT is a synthetic stress-test instantiating combinatorial qrel structure directly from the theorem: 50k documents, 1000 queries, k=2 relevant docs/query, simple natural-language attribute sentences (e.g., "Jon likes Hawaiian pizza, sports cars..."). A smaller "LIMIT-small" variant uses 46 documents specifically because C(46,2)=1035, "the smallest [choose-2 value] above 1k," giving one query per realizable pair. Results (verbatim): "in the full setting models struggle to reach even 20% recall@100 and in the 46 document version models cannot solve the task even with recall@20." Models tested include Gemini Embedding, Qwen3 Embedding, E5-Mistral-7B, GTE-ModernColBERT (multi-vector), Arctic-embed 2.0, and BM25 (lexical, which "does very well due to their higher [effective] dimensionality" and "comes close to perfect scores"). A free-embedding (unconstrained, directly-optimized) best-case experiment shows real optimization needs d>18 for a case where Theorem 1's lower bound is only 4 — "a 4.5 multiplier even in the no-generalization... case" — meaning practical/learned dimension requirements are markedly worse than the theoretical floor, not just marginally so. Training on a LIMIT training split does not fix the problem (rules out domain shift as the cause); overfitting directly to the test set does fix it, confirming the limitation is representational capacity, not distributional mismatch.

**Objective function used in Weller et al.'s own experiments (not a proposed method — for completeness).** The free-embedding optimization directly optimizes raw embedding vectors by gradient descent against a binary top-k-accuracy criterion (no architecture, no encoder). The LIMIT fine-tuning experiments use the SentenceTransformers library's standard **MultipleNegativesRankingLoss** (an off-the-shelf in-batch-negatives contrastive/ranking loss) with a "no duplicates" negative sampler — i.e., **the paper proposes no novel training objective at all**; its contribution is the impossibility theorem plus the LIMIT benchmark, not a quantization or training method.

**Practical implication stated by the authors (verbatim):** "the community should be aware of these limitations, both when creating evals and also by using alternate architectures — such as cross-encoders / multi-vector / more expressive similarity functions — when trying to handle the full range of instruction queries."

**Verdict — relevance to the target claim.** Weller et al. is **not a quantization or training-objective paper** and does not itself claim or attempt "joint rate-constrained quantization of encoder AND index." Its relevance is as a **hard ceiling / caveat**: for a fixed real-valued embedding dimension d (before any bit-quantization is even applied), there exist top-k document subsets no single-vector dot-product model can realize with any positive margin, scaling as d=Ω(k·log(en/k)/log(1+1/γ)); bit-quantizing that same d-dimensional space to a 64-512-bit budget can only tighten this ceiling further (more coordinate noise → effectively larger required γ → per Eq. 2, a *larger*, not smaller, d requirement is needed to hold the same margin), though the paper does not derive this bit-budget-specific version explicitly. The appendix's rank_rop=rank_rt identity for binary matrices is a genuinely useful supporting citation for treating "threshold-crossing preservation" and "ranking/top-k preservation" as the same target quantity. **No change to this file's earlier characterization of Weller et al. is needed beyond adding this exact-quote detail** — §3.14 and claim (5) in §4 already scoped this correctly as a caveat/bound, not a competing method.

## Prior art ke kvantizační větvi (doplněno 2026-09-06 po externí recenzi)

Dosavadní rešerše v tomto souboru pokrývá **starší linii** (destilace embeddingů, kvantizace indexu, ternární
enkodéry). Kvantizační větev má vlastní předchůdce a recenze správně upozornila, že tvrzení „literatura tuhle
úlohu skoro neřeší" neobstojí. Tři práce, které recenzent jmenoval a které je **potřeba ověřit a zapracovat**
(zatím neověřeno z primárního zdroje, uvedeno tak, jak bylo doporučeno):

| práce | co z našeho postupu předjímá | co tím z našich tvrzení padá |
|---|---|---|
| *On the Impact of Calibration Data* (studie vlivu kalibračních dat na kvantizaci LLM) | doménově odpovídající kalibrace je zavedené doporučení, ne náš nález | „kalibrace na cílové doméně pomáhá" **není nové**; naše přidaná hodnota je kvantifikace, jak efekt škáluje s poškozením kvantizátoru, a měření robustnosti (objem, přenos, zastarávání) |
| **QuIP#** | oboustranné Hadamardovy transformace pro odstranění nekoherence **a** vektorová kvantizace; jeho inferenční implementace provádí Hadamardovu transformaci **před i po** kvantovaném matmulu | „oboustranná rotace + VQ" **není náš nález** a **re-asociace `y = Aᵀ(W'_q(Bᵀx))` také ne** (druhá recenze, 2026-09-07). Naše zůstává: měření, kdy se to vyplatí; volba rotace per matici (nesdílené) pro weight-only kvantizaci; ověřený nasaditelný řetěz (faktorizace = matice, přeskládaný výpočet = diagnostika, AVX2 kernel = reference) |
| *Query Encoder Distillation via Embedding Alignment* | malý dotazový enkodér při zachování silného dokumentového enkodéru; řeší už i **kompatibilitu** malého dotazového enkodéru s původním dokumentovým | asymetrické nasazení **není nová úloha** a kompatibilita s indexem **není náš nález**; naše je reprodukovatelný postup komprese dotazové strany **bez doučování**, kalibrovaný z dostupného korpusu, s kvalitou ověřenou proti existujícímu indexu a s praktickým exportem |

**Důsledek pro formulaci:** vlastní příspěvek je *reprodukovatelný postup komprese dotazové strany bez
doučování, kalibrovaný z dostupného korpusu, s kvalitou ověřenou proti existujícímu indexu a s praktickým
exportem*, ne jednotlivé stavební kameny. Tvrzení o prvenství v `pitch.md` bylo staženo; po druhé recenzi
(2026-09-07) i tvrzení o re-asociaci jako algoritmické novince.

**Rozlišení, které v textech dosud chybělo:** GPTQ optimalizuje **rekonstrukci výstupu jednotlivých vrstev** na
kalibrační sadě. „Kalibrace na korpusu" tedy působí na rekonstrukční chybu vrstev; end-to-end retrieval metrika
(nDCG@10 proti fp indexu), kterou hlásíme, je toho důsledek, ne optimalizovaná veličina. Publikované práce
o kalibračních datech měří perplexitu na téže úrovni, na jaké GPTQ optimalizuje; my měříme o úroveň výš.

**Nedokončeno:** ověřit tyto tři práce z primárních zdrojů, zjistit, zda někdo měřil kalibrační efekt jako funkci
bitového rozpočtu (naše nejsilnější metodologická teze), a doplnit srovnání s `llama-quantize --imatrix`
na doménových datech jako nejbližší jednoduchou alternativou.

## Doplněk 2026-09-08 — mezery zjištěné před prvním zveřejněním (ověřeno na arXiv 2026-09-08)

Tři témata, která rešerše výše neměla vůbec, a bez nichž by zpráva působila, že o nich nevíme:

**Vektorová kvantizace vah s kódovými knihami.** *GPTVQ: The Blessing of Dimensionality for LLM Quantization*
(van Baalen et al., 2024, arXiv:2402.15319) — post-training VQ s hessiánsky vedeným střídáním kvantizace sloupců a
korekce zbytku; naše „GPTVQ-lite" (kódová kniha dim 4 na 256sloupcový blok, zmražené řádkové amplitudy) je zjednodušená
reimplementace téhož principu, ne nová metoda. *AQLM* (Egiazarian et al., 2024, arXiv:2401.06118) — aditivní kódové knihy
s doučováním, silnější, ale vyžaduje trénink. *QuIP#* (Tseng et al., 2024, arXiv:2402.04396) — Hadamardova inkoherence
+ E8 mřížkové knihy + doučení. Co je u nás jiné a doložené: cíl retrieval (nDCG proti nezměněnému indexu), žádné doučování,
vlastní WebGPU runtime kontejneru (`client/vqweb`), měření strany rotace per sazba.

**Rotace.** *QuaRot* (Ashkboos et al., 2024, arXiv:2404.00456) — Hadamardovy rotace pro 4bitovou kvantizaci vah,
aktivací i KV cache, sdílené rotace absorbované do sousedních vrstev; *SpinQuant* (Liu et al., 2024, arXiv:2405.16406,
ICLR 2025) — rotace učené na kalibračních datech. Naše rotace je jen na straně vah (weight-only, per matice, nesdílená,
zrušená online), tedy užší nastavení; jediný doložený rozdíl je měření, kdy se to na retrievalu vyplatí (silně poškozené
modely / sub-2-bitové mřížky), a negativní výsledek na silné mřížce (nestabilní přes seedy).

**Embedding inference v prohlížeči.** `transformers.js` (Hugging Face; ONNX Runtime Web, WASM/WebGPU) je standardní
cesta pro embedding modely v prohlížeči; `wllama` (ngxson; llama.cpp ve WASM, od 3.x s WebGPU backendem llama.cpp) je
to, co používá naše demo. Nic z prohlížečového runtimu skalárních klientů není naše; naše je jen měření (latence, paměť,
shoda s nativním během do ±0,003) a standalone WebGPU runtime pro VQ formát, který ani jeden z nich nemá. Neprovedli jsme
srovnání s ONNX int8/int4 exportem téhož modelu přes transformers.js — to je otevřená mezera pro zprávu (zapsáno v
`docs/release/release_plan.md`).
